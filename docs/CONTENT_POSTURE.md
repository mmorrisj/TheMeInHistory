# Content posture

Any serious historical setting reaches atrocity. Virginia 1607-1624 contains
dispossession, massacre in both directions, starvation severe enough to produce
documented cannibalism, indentured servitude enforced by the gallows, and the
first Africans landed in English North America by force.

The position this project takes is not to soften any of that and not to render
it for effect.

## Four categories, declared per pack

Every `pack.yaml` names them explicitly, and they are written into the
narrator's system prompt on every turn.

**Unavoidable** — what the era simply contains. Naming these up front means no
reader is ambushed and no generation has to decide on the fly.

**Depicts** — rendered on the page. Hunger, illness, the aftermath of violence
and its cost to the people who committed it, period contempt in period mouths
where the scene is about that contempt.

**Reports** — acknowledged as historical fact, never staged as a scene. Sexual
violence. The killing of the Paspahegh weroance's children in 1610, which George
Percy recorded in his own defence. Torture and execution under martial law.

**Refuses** — never written at all. Colonisation as adventure without cost to
the colonised. Enslaved or captive people depicted as content. Suffering
rendered for effect rather than understanding.

## Fixed outcomes

Documented atrocities are not player-editable and not gamified. The Powhatan
lose their land; the Starving Time kills most of the colony; Africans are landed
at Point Comfort in August 1619 and are not free to leave; Opechancanough's
attack falls on 22 March 1622.

When a player's choice would avert one of these, the story bends — they witness
it, are swept up in it, or arrive too late. The engine never breaks frame to
tell a reader what they may not do.

## Language

Period terms for people are handled by `contested_terms` in `language.yaml`. The
narrator's own voice never uses period slurs. English characters may, in
dialogue, where the scene is about English attitudes and where the prose lets it
land as the judgement it is. Scattering them for colour is forbidden.

Direct quotation of a source is different. Rolfe's "20. and odd Negroes" appears
verbatim in the timeline because it is the document, and confronting how those
people entered the written record is the point.

## Where the record is silent

The pack is explicit about what nobody wrote down. Mistress Forrest's first name
is unrecorded — do not invent one. Angelo appears in the 1625 muster as a single
line with no age and no status. Englishwomen's daily work is reconstructed
because the men who wrote everything down did not consider it worth describing.

Marking these as `reconstruction` rather than `fact` in the ledger is how the
reader learns that the silences in history are themselves evidence.

## Age modes

Planned, not yet built. They will change depiction depth and never historical
honesty. A family mode may decline to render a scene; it will not pretend the
thing did not happen.
